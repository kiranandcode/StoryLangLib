Mara squeezed her arm, checked her reflection in the dark window, and was gone, the stairwell door booming once down the corridor. Kiran sat. She pulled her notebook toward her and wrote the date at the top of a fresh page, then only looked at it. The tea was too hot to drink and she drank it anyway, letting it scald, which was at least a sensation with a clear cause. Above her, through four floors of concrete, two people she liked were standing in the wind. She told herself she was glad for Mara, and found that this was true, and that it did not help. She relabelled a tube she had already relabelled. The clock over the door said ten fifty-eight.
