He turned to face her properly. "Kiran," he said. "Can I kiss you?" It was so plainly put that she felt it land in her body before her mind caught up, a warmth under her ribs, the cold suddenly beside the point. She had wanted this, she realised, for longer than she had admitted even to her notebook. Still, she wanted the asking to go both ways. "Yes," she said. "And you want to? You're sure?" "Yes," he said, and smiled. "I'm sure. I've been sure for three weeks." She stepped closer, close enough to see that the wind had reddened the tips of his ears, and found she was not afraid at all.
