She was the one who closed the distance. Kiran put a hand flat against his coat, felt the wool cold on the outside and his warmth coming through it, and kissed him. His mouth tasted faintly of the cheap peppermints from the jar by the lab door. He was careful at first, as if she might change her mind, and then less careful, his hand rising to the back of her neck where her hair had come loose. She noticed absurd things: gravel shifting under her heel, a siren somewhere south of the river, the fact that he was humming, very slightly, against her lips. She laughed into it and did not pull away. Below them the city went on blinking, and the roof door, propped on its brick, let the night in.
