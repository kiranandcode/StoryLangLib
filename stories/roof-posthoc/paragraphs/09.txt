The stairwell smelled of dust and floor polish, and her footsteps came back to her off the walls, slightly late. Kiran counted landings. Third floor, fourth. *There's something I should have said.* She turned the sentence over the way she turned tubes in her fingers, looking for the label that would tell her what was inside. On the last flight the air cooled; someone had propped the fire door with a brick. She pushed through and the city opened under her: the orange grid of streetlights, a night bus sliding along the river road, the hospital tower with its red aircraft lamp blinking, patient and slow. Her hands were cold. She could still turn back. She knew she would never forgive herself for not knowing.
