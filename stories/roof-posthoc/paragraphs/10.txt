Theo was at the railing where Mara must have left him, and he straightened when he saw her. Kiran crossed the gravel and stopped an arm's length away, the wind pulling her hair loose. "Was it for me?" she asked. Her voice came out smaller than she wanted. "Yes," Theo said, with no hesitation in it at all. "I should have said it the first week. I like you, Kiran. Not the polite way. I've been coming back for cables I didn't forget since about day three." He laughed at himself, briefly. "I didn't want to leave without you knowing." She watched the red lamp instead of him, because it was easier. "I suppose," she said, "I might not be quite as glad you're leaving as I said."
