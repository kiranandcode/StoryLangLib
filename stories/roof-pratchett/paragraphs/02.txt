"Don't flatter yourself," Kiran said. "I'm counting the hours until you're gone. Tomorrow I get my corner back." This was a lie of the transparent sort, the kind that people tell with the whole face, and she heard the transparency herself, a moment too late. Ran did not take the bait. He had known her since she was nine, and he had never once taken the bait. He tipped his head as though she had said something kind, and a grin arrived on his face by degrees, like a sunrise that has been told there is no hurry. "You'll miss me," he said. "You did last time." "Not even a little." She saved the lemma into the wrong file and had to cut it out again, and he laughed, softly, as if she had answered a different question.
