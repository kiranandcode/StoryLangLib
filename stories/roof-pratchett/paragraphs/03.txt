"Tea," Kiran said, already standing, because her face was doing something she did not want him to watch. "Two minutes. Don't touch my laptop." She carried her mug down the corridor to the kitchen and stood over the kettle for longer than it needed, which is the traditional use of a kettle by people who are not ready to go back into a room.* In the lab, Ran tore a page from the back of his notebook. He wrote fast and crossed nothing out: "Roof. Eleven. Like before. There's something I should have said. —R" He set it square on her keyboard, where she could not miss it, pushed the chair in, and took the stairs up two at a time, the way a man runs for the Last Train.
* Kettles know this, and take longer on purpose. Nobody has ever caught a kettle in the act.
