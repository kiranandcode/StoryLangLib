"If this is about the flashlight," she said, "I want it back." He laughed, and then he didn't. "It was never old times, Kiran. I should have said it before my family moved, and I was fourteen and stupid and I didn't. I liked you. I've liked you all week, and I think I've liked you for ten years, which is embarrassing." She waited for the punchline. There is always a punchline; the universe is built that way, out of banana skins and timing.* It did not come. He just stood there with the city behind him, looking at her as if she were the only lit window in it, and something in her chest that had been braced for years did not know what to do with itself.
* Kiran had been the butt of the universe's jokes since she was nine, and she had learned to laugh first. A laugh saves time.
