She was the one who closed the distance, because if she waited she would talk herself out of it, and she had been talking herself out of things for most of her life.* She took his face in her hands and kissed him. He was warm and tasted faintly of the rain, and he made a small surprised sound against her mouth, like a man who has opened a door expecting a cupboard and found a summer. Then he held her as though he had been planning where his hands would go for ten years. When she pulled back to breathe, he kept his forehead against hers, and for once in her life she did not reach for the joke. She let him hold her. Below them the city went on with the business of being lit, and did not look up, which was decent of it.
* A great deal of nonsense is talked about Love, mostly by people with something to sell. The truth is smaller and better: somebody asks, somebody says yes, and both mean every word.
