For example, suppose we use k=2, and m=3. First, observe that as Bloom correctly derives, the probability of any single bit being raised after an insertion is 1/3. Now, if after inserting 1 element, we learn that bit b1 is raised, then this means that with probability 1/2, either b2 or b3 is raised (from the from that hash outputs must be distinct). As such, the bits are not independent: P[ b2 is raised | b1 is raised ] != P[ b2 is raised] (1/2 != 1/3).
