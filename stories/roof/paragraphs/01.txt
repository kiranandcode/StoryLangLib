At ten the computer lab was down to Kiran and the printers ticking as they cooled. She had the corner desk, where the two rows by the window sat back to back, and every lamp but hers was off, so the dividers made a small warm room of her end of it; the big table on the far side of the desks had gone to shadow. Then the door clicked, and Ran came back in with rain on his shoulders. "Forgot my scarf," he said, and did not look for it. He took the desk on her left, the one he had borrowed all week, where the divider stopped short of the chair and nothing stood between them, and watched her type. She lost her place in the proof. "Your last night," she said, "and you spend it here." "Fifteen years, Kiran. The hotel bar can wait." He said it plainly, the way he had said everything since he was nine, and smiled at her until she had to look up.
