The roof door stuck, then gave, and Mara stepped out into rain that had thinned to a fine cold mist. Theo was by the ventilation stacks, hands in his coat pockets, and he turned at the sound with his whole face ready. She watched it change when he saw her: not disappointment exactly, but a recalculation, quick and kind. She held up the paper anyway. "Was this for me?" He looked at it for a long moment, and she liked him for not answering fast. "No," he said. "I'm sorry, Mara. It was for Kiran." The mist beaded on her sleeves. She thought of the turnstile, of *remind me*, and understood all at once what he had been meaning to tell her, and that it had never been about her at all. "Oh," she said. "Of course it was."
