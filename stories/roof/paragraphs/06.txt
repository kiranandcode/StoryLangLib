Mara left at twenty to eleven, after checking her reflection in the dark window and laughing at herself for it. "Wish me luck," she said from the door, and Kiran did, and meant most of it. Then the corridor took her footsteps, and somewhere far off the stairwell door boomed once. Kiran pulled the rack toward her and uncapped her pen. The labels were already right. She traced over a 7 until the ink pooled and bled into the frost. His gloves lay inside out at the edge of the bench, fingers curled toward her like something asleep. She did not turn them right side out. The second mug had gone cold beside hers, a skin forming on the tea. Overhead the rain changed pitch, and she found she was listening for voices through four floors of concrete, which was impossible, and which she kept doing anyway.
