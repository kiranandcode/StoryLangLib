Mara had made it as far as the bus shelter before she remembered the charger, still coiled behind her monitor where it always was, and she came back through the rain swearing softly at herself. The lab was empty, lights on, the hood breathing. Someone's tea things were missing from the shelf. She found the cable, and then she found the note, a torn triangle of weighing paper squared neatly on the bench that ran between her desk and Kiran's. *Roof. Eleven.* She read it standing, then sitting on her stool, the paper damp where her thumb held it. Theo had walked her to the station last Thursday and said, at the turnstile, "Remind me to tell you something," and then the train had come. She had thought about that sentence more than she would admit to Kiran. She looked at the clock: 10:14. Her coat was still dripping. She wanted, very much, to go up.
